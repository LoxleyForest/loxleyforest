Gold Rush, and golden hour

There's lots to do and lots going on downtown this weekend. Gold Rush Days fills the streets around the square, and it's a real festival. If you're going into town, get there early. The traffic can be pretty bad, but there's lots going on.

On your way up

Gold Rush Days, if you like

Around 300 art, craft and food vendors line the streets around the historic square, with live music, gold panning, children's activities, traditional Appalachian demonstrations, and a parade. Free to walk in. Thirteen-year-olds tend to find plenty to like.

Or the quiet way: if you'd rather have quiet and adventure, the forest is all yours. Gold Rush draws the crowds into town and often leaves the trails and waterfalls quieter than usual. Come straight to us and get the first taste of fall color while everybody else is at the festival.

The forest is yours

Gold Rush keeps the crowds on the square today, and that's your chance. Go get the best hike in reach while the trails are quiet, then taste a different festival on the way back.

9:30 am

Raven Cliff Falls

Dodd Creek runs beside you the whole way, spilling over cascade after cascade, until the trail ends at a waterfall pouring straight through a split in a cliff face. Two point six miles each way, and a great one for strong thirteen-year-old legs. Pack a lunch and eat it by the creek.

~35 to 40 min drive~5.2 mi round tripModerate$5 per carDirections
Know before: expect wet stream crossings early on, so wear shoes you don't mind getting wet. No climbing on the rocks at the falls. After a dry week, the water should be low and easy.
Afternoon

An encore, or a taste of Oktoberfest

You're already on the road to Helen, the little Bavarian-style mountain town, and its Oktoberfest is on this weekend, with German food and music at the Festhalle. Sample it on the way home from the falls. Or take the quiet encore: Dukes Creek Falls is minutes away on the same scenic highway, a walk down to a beautiful falls with observation decks along the way. And if the twins want more of Gold Rush, Sunday afternoon is a second chance at the square. Same advice either way: give the traffic some room.

Keep your eyes open

In the woods

October is when this forest is most alive. Everything is getting ready for winter at once, and if you walk slowly and listen, it will show you.

The bees
Right here

Our bees are buzzing at the hives, working the last of the season's flowers before the cold sets in.

Goldenrod
Look for yellow

Bright yellow plumes along the edges of the woods and the roads, one of the bees' great late feasts. And no, it isn't what makes you sneeze. That's ragweed.

Witch hazel
Smell for it

The strangest bloom in the forest. Just as the leaves start to fall, it opens spidery yellow flowers along the creek banks and the edges of the woods, with a sweet scent on cool air.

The pileated woodpecker
Look up

Crow-sized, with a flaming red crest. Old-timers call it the Lord God bird, because that's what you say when you see one.

Deer, and maybe a bear
Dawn & dusk

Early morning and the last light are your best chance to see deer at their most active. If you're lucky, you might catch a black bear in the morning light. If you do, give it plenty of room and enjoy it from a distance.

The night shift
After dark

Lie still and listen. The barred owl asks its question through the trees, "Who cooks for you? Who cooks for you all?" And now and then the coyotes join in the symphony.

Your home base

The treehouse

Everything you need to know about the place you'll keep coming back to all weekend.

The bridge

A hundred feet of rope bridge sixty feet up, with the creek somewhere below you. You'll hear it before you see it. Cross it again after dark with the lights on. It's a completely different walk at night.

The sky deck

Home to the oven, the fire table, and the 101-inch screen that rolls down for movie night, with the forest just starting to turn. Pizza is a Saturday plan.

The fire pit, after the storm

Last weekend's hurricane brought high winds and heavy rain to the mountain, and it blew down a dead tree that took a massive oak down with it, right over the fire pit. That oak was a century old and three feet across, and a few other trees came down beside it. The good news is there will be lots of firewood for all. We're working as fast as we can to clear it so the pit is open and ready for you this weekend, but it's possible it will have to stay closed while we finish. We'd rather you know now. If it is closed, the fire table on your sky deck is right there for roasting marshmallows under the trees. When the pit is open, build your fire the honest way, please: no gas and no accelerants.

Staying warm

There's a little heater to cut the chill and extra blankets for the night, with fans if the afternoon sun makes it toasty. Nights in the trees run cool this time of year, and you'll be cozy.

The forest kitchen

Your oven on the sky deck is a Gozney, and it's more than a pizza oven. Top chefs cook all kinds of things in one, from steak and eggs to burgers, and we've left you a skillet so you can too. Down at the fire pit there's a grill that hammers right into the eight-foot pit: build your fire, let it burn down to hot coals, and away you go. Steaks, hot dogs, whatever you can imagine. Plus a French press and coffee, an electric kettle, and a cooler on the deck.

Staying connected

Cell service is thin up here, which is part of the point. Wi-Fi runs from the hotspot on the deck.
