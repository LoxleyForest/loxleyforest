Drawn from the house

Hung in five oaks, lit from inside

  • Five oaks hold the house. Two on each side and one behind it. The front right oak runs up through the deck, as it does in the photograph.
  • Every cable starts at the truss. The frame under the deck ends in two bottom hubs, and each cable runs from a hub up to a collar on an oak. Nothing ties to the spire tops.
  • The canvas glows. The spires are the brightest lines in the drawing, and the bridge and rail carry their string lights.
  • The fly window. A canvas panel that follows the lines of the canvas, pointed at the top and lit from inside, with the zipper down its centre. (Mick's note, 13 October.)
  • A sketch artist's hand. The canvas lines swell and thin as the pen presses and eases, lift now and then, and carry light cross-hatching where the glow is strongest. (Livy's note, 13 October.)
  • Open air below. The deck sits high, with ferns on the forest floor far beneath.
  • An acorn crowns the arch. For the five oaks, and to echo the gold finials on the spires.
  • The arrow stays. It runs through the name as it does in the current logo, now with drawn feathers.

Two icons

A lean one for the browser tab, the engraving for the phone

The favicon is the little picture in a browser tab. It is drawn lean so it stays crisp from 16 pixels up: the arch, the two spires, the deck and the cables from the hub. The app icon is what a guest sees when they save the site to their phone's home screen, so it carries the whole engraving.
