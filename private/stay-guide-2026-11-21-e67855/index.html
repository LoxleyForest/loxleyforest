Up the trail and across

You arrive, you cross, and the weekend starts. Nothing on today's list is required.

From 3:00 pm

Your treehouse is ready

Drive in between the two signs. Just past where the paved drive dips downhill you'll pass the hives, our bee yard, and right after them the dirt road on the right: Bobcat Road, narrow and bumpy, about six hundred feet to the parking area. The trail starts beside it. Five to ten minutes under the trees, and around the last curve the laurel opens up and there's the bridge. That bridge is the front door. We'll send the drive, the walk and the crossing the day before, step by step.

When you get there

Cross the bridge

A hundred feet of suspended bridge, sixty feet up, over the holler, with a creek running somewhere below. Take it slow the first time. It sways, and it's built to. Get across before the light goes: sunset is 5:28.

Evening

Dinner in town, booked ahead

Downtown Dahlonega is about ten minutes. For a great burger, Spirits Tavern on the square, open until 11. For a sit-down dinner in town, Public House on North Grove Street. And Montaluce, about twenty minutes away, is Mick's favorite: the restaurant is the real draw. Its last seating Saturday is 9 pm.

The TreeMail box

Fresh pastries arrive in the TreeMail box, on a post in the trees where you first stepped onto the bridges. Getting them means crossing both bridges, the short one and the hundred-footer. It's the nicest walk to breakfast you'll ever take.

Amicalola Falls
The big one

The tallest cascading waterfall in Georgia, 729 feet. The loop trail is 2.1 miles with a lot of stairs to the best views; from the top, a staircase of 425 steps runs down the falls. For the easy view, park at the West Ridge Access Trail: a paved, accessible path leads to the middle of the falls.

Your home base

The treehouse

Everything you need to know about the place you'll keep coming back to all weekend.

The bridge

A hundred feet of suspended bridge over the holler, with the creek running below. Cross it again after dark with the lights on.

The forest kitchen

No fridge and no stove, and you won't miss them. There's a sink, cutlery, an electric kettle, a French press and coffee. The big cooler on the deck is your fridge once you add ice. The pizza oven on the Sky Deck takes a cast iron pan that does anything from bacon and eggs to a steak. Down at the Fire Circle there's a stainless steel grill over the fire, and your own Dutch oven is welcome on the fire too.

The shower

A rainfall shower, the head fifteen feet up. The water comes from our own well: the treehouse holds about 55 gallons, roughly a fifteen-minute shower, and we'll top it up whenever you ask.

Power and warmth

The treehouse runs on a battery system, so please no electric heaters or heated blankets. For warmth there's the propane fireplace and extra blankets, and on the deck the fire table runs on propane: unlatch the front panel and light it with a lighter.

Movie night

The screen on the deck rail is raised and lowered with the window extension pole, and it can come down fast. The projector and the streaming stick plug into the power bar, and the streaming accounts are signed in.

Lights

The switches are on the wall to the right as you come up the stairs, with a dimmer for the chandelier over the bed. The bridge and deck lights run on the sun, two to three hours after dark.

Windows

They work like tent windows: the canvas rolls up and ties back, and the extension pole with the hook reaches the high zipper.

Staying connected

Cell service is thin up here, which is part of the point. The Wi-Fi is a hotspot on the battery system on the deck, about the size of a phone. Switch it on.
