Canopy life

The fireplace has a personality

The treehouse is warmed by a propane fireplace, and on a cold night it sometimes wants a little coaxing to light. Think of it as the one resident of the forest who likes to be asked twice. Here is the whole trick.

Lighting the pilot

Below the firebox there's a metal grate door. Open it, and you'll find a knob and an ignition button.

  1. Turn the knob to pilot.
  2. Press and hold the knob down for about 10 to 15 seconds, so the propane makes its way up to the pilot.
  3. Keep holding it down, and at the same time press the ignition button.
  4. Keep pressing the ignition button until the pilot flame is lit.
  5. Sometimes it needs you to keep holding for another 15 or 20 seconds. If it doesn't catch, start again from step 1 until it does. Sometimes it takes two or three tries. That's normal up here.

If you ever smell gas, turn the knob to off, open a window, wait a few minutes, and send us a message on the group text before you try again.

Up the trail, across, and pizza

Your treehouse is ready

Drive in between the two signs. Just past where the paved drive dips downhill you'll pass the hives, our bee yard, and right after them the dirt road on the right: Bobcat Road, narrow and bumpy, about six hundred feet to the parking area. The trail starts beside it. Five to ten minutes under the trees, and around the last curve the laurel opens up and there's the bridge. That bridge is the front door. When you get there

Cross the bridge

A hundred feet of suspended bridge, sixty feet up, over the holler, with a creek running somewhere below. Take it slow the first time. It sways, and it's built to. Get across before the light goes: sunset is 5:33.

Evening

Pizza on the Sky Deck

The dough is waiting in the proofer. The oven is a Gozney Roccbox on the Sky Deck, sixty feet above the forest floor.

  1. Preheat. Push in the dial and turn it to light the oven, then turn it up to HIGH. Walk away for 15 minutes.
  2. Start with the small dough. It's your test run. If it's perfect, you've earned an appetizer. If it burns, you've learned exactly how fast this oven cooks and saved your real pizza.
  3. Roll it out on the parchment, from the middle out. Keep it a size you can slide off the peel and turn easily.
  4. Sauce only, first. A light coat of sauce, nothing else. Give it the shimmy test on the peel: if it sticks, work the edge loose before you launch. Cook about 1 minute 30 seconds.
  5. Pull it out and dress it. Cheese, about half what your instincts say, then your toppings. Turn the flame down to HALF.
  6. Back in, and turn it. Wait for the crust to rise and color, then rotate every 20 to 30 seconds with the small peel. No more than 2 minutes in all. You're looking for leopard spots and bubbling cheese.
  7. Pull, and turn the oven back to HIGH for the next one.
  8. When you're done, push in the dial and turn it to off. The oven and the stone stay hot a long time.

Three waterfalls

Amicalola Falls
The big one

The tallest cascading waterfall in Georgia, 729 feet. The loop trail is 2.1 miles with a lot of stairs to the best views; from the top, a staircase of 425 steps runs down the falls. For the easy view, park at the West Ridge Access Trail: a paved, accessible path leads to the middle of the falls.

~45 min · open every day in daylight hours · the visitor center 8 to 5 · a Georgia State Parks pass is needed to park

The long one

2.6 miles each way to the falls, with stream crossings: the Forest Service says to be ready to get your feet wet. On a frosty weekend, save it for a warmer visit, or bring dry socks.

Your home base

The treehouse

Everything you need to know about the place you'll keep coming back to all weekend.

The bridge

A hundred feet of suspended bridge over the holler, with the creek running below. Cross it again after dark with the lights on.

The forest kitchen

No fridge and no stove, and you won't miss them. There's a sink, cutlery, an electric kettle (under the counter), a French press and coffee. The big cooler on the deck is your fridge once you add ice. The pizza oven on the Sky Deck takes a cast iron pan that does anything from bacon and eggs to a steak. Down at the Fire Circle there's a stainless steel grill over the fire.

The shower and the water

A rainfall shower, the head fifteen feet up. The water comes from our own well: the treehouse holds about 55 gallons, roughly a fifteen-minute shower, and we'll top it up whenever you ask. On a cold night the water is off from 6 pm until it's back up to 40 degrees.

Power and warmth

The treehouse runs on a battery system, so please no electric heaters or heated blankets. For warmth there's the propane fireplace (its steps are at the top of this guide) and extra blankets, and on the deck the fire table runs on propane: unlatch the front panel and light it with a lighter.

Movie night

The screen on the deck rail is raised and lowered with the window extension pole, and it can come down fast. The projector and the streaming stick plug into the power bar, and the streaming accounts are signed in.

Lights

The switches are on the wall to the right as you come up the stairs, with a dimmer for the chandelier over the bed. The trail and bridge lights run on the sun, and stay on two to four hours after dark, depending on how much sunlight they saw that day.

Windows

They work like tent windows: the canvas rolls up and ties back, and the extension pole with the hook reaches the high zipper.

Staying connected

Cell service is thin up here, which is part of the point. The Wi-Fi is a hotspot on the battery system on the deck, about the size of a phone. Switch it on.
