What makes it different

Built, not bought

Built by the family that lives here

The treehouse is a tetrahedron frame, steel sandwiched between wood, suspended from living oaks by specialized hardware. Canvas roofs instead of timber keep the load on the trees as light as possible. Every piece was carried down four hundred feet of mountain without a single machine touching the forest.

It took nine months, three times longer than planned, because in a living forest the trees sway, the terrain fights you, and the forest has the final say.

Sixty feet changes everything

At ground level, a forest is beautiful. At sixty feet, it's transcendent. You're level with the hawks. Sound works differently up here: the wind moves through the leaves like a living thing, and the silence between gusts is the deepest quiet you've heard in years.

The crossing

The hundred-foot rope bridge isn't a gimmick. It's a threshold. The moment you step onto it, you leave the ground behind. By the time you reach the other side, something in your chest has shifted.
