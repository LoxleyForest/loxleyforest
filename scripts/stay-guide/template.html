Close to the treehouse

Waterfalls

Stay on built trails and platforms and keep off wet rock at the edge of the falls. After a big rain they run loud, and the crossings run high.

Raven Cliff Falls
{{Dry day only}}

About 5 miles round trip along Dodd Creek to a falls splitting a cliff. The best hike nearby, with an unbridged crossing early on, so not the day after a big rain.

~35 to 40 min · moderate · $5
Dukes Creek Falls
Any day

A shorter walk down to a beautiful falls on the Richard Russell Scenic Highway.

~35 min · sunrise to sunset · $4 per car
DeSoto Falls
{{After rain}}

The closest of the good ones, with an easy walk.

~20 min · open 24 hours · $5 per car
Anna Ruby Falls
{{After rain}}

Twin waterfalls at the end of a short paved path near Helen.

~45 min · 9am to 5pm, last entry 4pm · $6 per person 16 and up
Amicalola Falls
Know before

Georgia's tallest cascading falls at 729 feet, reached by a long staircase. The East Ridge Trail is closed for maintenance this year, so the full loop isn't possible.

~30 to 35 min · $10 per car

Your home base

The treehouse

Everything you need to know about the place you'll keep coming back to all weekend.

The bridge

A hundred feet of suspended rope bridge over the holler, with the creek running below. It sways, and it's built to. Cross it again after dark with the lights on. It's a completely different walk at night.

The sky deck

Home to the pizza oven and the 101-inch screen that rolls down for movie night, sixty feet up, with the forest changing by the week. {{Pizza is a Friday plan this weekend, while it's dry.}}

The fire

The fire pit is at ground level in the trees. Build it the honest way, please: no gas and no accelerants.

The forest kitchen

French press, electric kettle, cast iron, and a Yeti cooler on the deck with the ice taken care of. Step-by-step help for the oven is in the pizza guide.

Staying connected

Cell service is thin up here, which is part of the point. Wi-Fi runs from the hotspot on the deck.
