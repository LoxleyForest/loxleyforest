The crossing

This is not a cabin with a view. This is a home in the sky, suspended in a canopy of white oak and beech that has stood for over a century.

You'll cross a thirty-foot rope bridge through the trees as a warm-up.

Then the hundred-foot bridge appears,

the one that makes your heart beat faster

before the forest slows it back down.

That one takes you to your front door.

The treehouse

Everything you need. Nothing you don't.

You'll fall asleep sixty feet in the air with nothing above you but stars and nothing below you but the creek rolling through the forest floor.

  • A queen bed framed by panoramic windows
  • A private deck above the forest floor
  • Fire & Flour: fresh dough and a wood-fired oven on your sky deck
  • TreeMail: morning coffee and seasonal treats, delivered
  • A pop-up projector for cinema night in the canopy
  • A fire pit on the forest floor
  • Heated for the cold seasons, with windows that open to the forest in summer
  • A kitchen for morning coffee and midnight snacks
  • 42 acres of cove forest to explore

What we don't include: televisions. You won't need one.

Built by hand

A family that lives in the forest they built

They carried every beam and board down four hundred feet of mountain by hand, because they wouldn't let a machine touch the forest. The arborists said the rope bridge was too long. They built it anyway.

They live here now: Mick, Livy, Aurora and Tilly Mac. When you arrive, the people who designed and built your treehouse are the same people handing you a welcome basket.
