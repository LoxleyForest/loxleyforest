The treehouse

A home in the sky

This is not a cabin with a view. This is a home in the sky, suspended in a canopy of white oak and beech that has stood for over a century.

A queen bed framed by panoramic windows that turn every morning into a painting you've never seen before. A private deck suspended above the forest floor. A pizza oven on your sky deck, because even pizza is better in the canopy.

What we don't include: televisions. You won't need one.

The evening

Your stay is a story, not a checklist

Unpack. Step onto the deck. Realize you're sixty feet in the air and there isn't a single sound except wind through leaves.

Fire up the pizza oven on the sky deck. Open the bottle you brought. Burn the first pizza. Laugh about it. Nail the second one.

Build a fire. Roast marshmallows. Look up.

Fall asleep to absolute silence, sixty feet above the forest floor. Wake up to birdsong. Check the TreeMail box at the entrance to your bridge. Something is waiting inside.

The crossing

Your heart beats faster

The thirty-foot bridge is the warm-up. Next come your first steps across the hundred-footer. The ground drops away. The trees rise to meet you. Your hands grip the ropes and your heart beats faster. Halfway across, you stop noticing the height and start noticing the silence.

Sit with the person you love and hear nothing but the creek and the wind in the canopy. Sound works differently up here: the wind moves through the leaves like a living thing, and the silence between gusts is the deepest quiet you've heard in years.

Built by hand

Built, not bought

The treehouse is a tetrahedron frame, steel sandwiched between wood, suspended from living oaks by specialized hardware. Every piece was carried down four hundred feet of mountain without a single machine touching the forest. It took nine months, three times longer than planned.

The deck planks are white oak, salvaged and milled from standing dead trees on the property. Wood that was already here, given a second life.

How many people can say they've spent the night hanging from five oak trees, sixty feet in the air, with nothing between them and the stars but canvas and canopy?

Questions

Everything you're wondering

Can we celebrate an anniversary here?

Possibly the best place to do it. When you send us your dates, the form asks what brings you to Loxley, and anniversary is one of the answers.

What if one of us is afraid of heights?

Some of our best reviews come from guests who were terrified. The bridge is built to feel safe: the ropes are chest-high, the planks are solid, and the sway is gentle. Most guests who are nervous on the first crossing are running across it by the second.

Is the treehouse heated?

Heated for the cold seasons, with windows that open to the forest in summer.

Can we bring our dog?

Yes. Up to two well-behaved dogs are welcome, and there is no fee. Dogs only, please. Know the way in before you decide: the hundred-foot rope bridge sways, and it sits sixty feet up.

How do we book?

Book direct with the family who built it. Choose your dates and the total appears, taxes and cleaning included.
