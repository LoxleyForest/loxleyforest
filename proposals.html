Proposals at Loxley Forest

Ask the question sixty feet up

A hundred-foot rope bridge strung with small warm lights, in a North Georgia forest just over an hour from Atlanta.

Plan your proposalWhere to ask

The question

Halfway across the bridge, you stop noticing the height and start noticing the silence. That is a good place to ask.

Loxley Forest is 42 acres of hardwood cove forest above Dahlonega. The treehouse sits at the far end of the bridge, glowing from the inside like a lantern somebody lit for you.

Ask on the bridge at golden hour, by the fire after dark, or on the deck with nothing around you but trees. Tell us which, and we'll help you get the moment right.

You bring the ring. We'll set the scene.

Where to ask

On the bridge, by the fire, or in the canopy

Two more, set up just for you

How it works

Leave the setting to us. Keep the words for yourself.

  1. First

    Tell us the date

    Tell us the date you have in mind and how you want it to feel. Golden hour on the bridge or a fire after dark. Quiet, or with a photographer there to catch it.

  2. Then

    We set the scene

    Things like flowers, lanterns and champagne on ice, ready where you plan to ask.

  3. And then

    You ask

    The rest of the night is yours, in the treehouse at the far end of the bridge.

After the yes

Stay the night. Wake up engaged in the trees.

The treehouse is yours until morning: a queen bed framed by panoramic windows, a private deck above the forest floor, and the forest all around you.

For the morning, add the Dawn Table: breakfast carried across the bridge to your door, with a late checkout to go with it.

Questions

Before you ask

Can we keep it a secret?

Yes. We plan it with you and no one else. To your partner it can be a night away in the trees, right up until you ask.

Can we have a photographer there?

Yes. We coordinate with a photographer you choose, or one we suggest, so they know where to be and when.

How far is it from Atlanta?

Just over an hour. We're in the foothills above Dahlonega. The last mile is a mountain road. That's how you know you're close.
