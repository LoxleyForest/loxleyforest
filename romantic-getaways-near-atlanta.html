A treehouse sixty feet up, just over an hour from Atlanta

A hand-built treehouse sixty feet up in the canopy of a North Georgia cove forest, reached by a hundred-foot rope bridge, on 42 acres in Dahlonega, just over an hour from Atlanta.

What a stay is like

Sixty feet changes everything

You'll fall asleep sixty feet in the air with nothing above you but stars and nothing below you but the creek rolling through the forest floor.

At ground level, a forest is beautiful. At sixty feet, it's transcendent. You're level with the hawks. Sound works differently up here: the wind moves through the leaves like a living thing, and the silence between gusts is the deepest quiet you've heard in years.

The canopy at sunrise isn't a view. It's a slow-motion event.

Evenings

Stars and s'mores, a fire you built

Graham crackers, chocolate, marshmallows, and a fire you built yourself. The stars do the rest.

Every season has a story

Fall color. Winter fires.

In spring, wildflowers blanket the forest floor and mountain laurel blooms along every trail. The waterfalls run full, and the forest smells like rain and new growth.

In summer, fireflies light the forest at dusk: in June and July the canopy lights up from the inside. Long evenings on the deck. The canopy is so thick the only light is green.

In fall, color at elevation arrives earlier and lasts longer. The canopy turns gold, then crimson, then amber. The most dramatic season, and the most popular. Book early.

In winter, the bare canopy shows views the leaves keep secret, and the fire pit feels essential.

The bridge

One hundred feet between two white oaks

A hundred feet long. Sixty feet up. It hangs between two white oaks over the holler, strung with small warm lights that come on at dark and burn for two or three hours. The planks are white oak, salvaged and milled from standing dead trees on this land. A crew of riggers from North Carolina strung its first cables.

It sways. That's by design. The ropes are chest-high and the planks are solid. It's rated for eight people at one time, everyone counted, and that number is the engineer's, not ours.

Every guest crosses it. It isn't optional. It's the way home. For a wedding it's the aisle.

Who we are

Built by the family that lives here

We're Mick and Livy. We found this land on a cold January morning, with fog still burning off the valley, down a driveway lined with mountain laurel. Two hundred feet in, before we'd even seen the house, Livy said, “This is it. Come on.”

Mick saw the engineering. Livy saw the experience. Every beam was carried four hundred feet down the mountain by hand, because we loved the forest too much to bring in machines. Mick built much of the framing himself. Livy insisted on a full bathroom sixty feet up, because she wasn't climbing down a tree in the middle of the night, and neither should anyone else.

We live here, with two little girls who think rope bridges are a normal way to get home. Book direct and you deal with the family who built it.

Questions

Everything you're wondering

How many guests can stay?

The treehouse sleeps 4 comfortably, and up to 6 guests in all. Children are welcome.

Is there WiFi?

Yes. But we encourage you not to use it.

What time are check-in and check-out?

Check-in is from 3:00 PM and check-out is by 11:00 AM.

Is there a television?

No. What we don't include: televisions. You won't need one.

Is it hot up there in summer?

In summer the canopy itself is the climate system: the cove forest runs cooler than the valley floor, and the windows open to a cross-breeze sixty feet up. No compressor hum, no recycled air, just a forest that's been cooling itself for a few hundred years. You're suspended inside it.

Can we bring our dog?

Up to two well-behaved dogs are welcome, with no fee; dogs only. We want every guest, two-legged and four-legged, to be safe and comfortable at sixty feet.
