The sky over Loxley

Every night through mid-March, worked out for the treehouse deck: when the sun goes, when the bridge lights come on, where the moon stands, and how dark it gets. Drag the evening forward and watch it happen.

Light for pictures

Golden hour is the sun within six degrees of the horizon: warm, low, long shadows. Blue hour is the short deep-blue window just outside it, when the bridge lights and the lit canvas balance the sky. Times are for the deck; a ridge or the canopy can take the sun a few minutes early.

Who is out tonight

What a guest is most likely to hear or see on the chosen night, and when to listen. Nobody can promise an animal. Each line comes from a wildlife agency or a published study, named beside it; several are from the states next door, and none is yet the family's own record of this forest.

Nights the sky puts on a show

Meteor showers on their usual peak nights (the exact hour shifts a little each year), with that night's moon over the deck.

Sun and moon computed for the treehouse's own latitude and longitude with a standard ephemeris. Clouds are not in it: no one can promise weather. A dark-sky score of 100 means no moon at all between 8 and 11 PM.
